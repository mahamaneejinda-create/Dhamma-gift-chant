Purpose

This space is offered as a Dhamma gift so that interested people may study and use the chants with wholesome intention. Its aim is peace, safety, goodwill, and non-harming.

Compiled and arranged by Ajarn Pol Mahamaneejinda.

Preface

This collection of chants and ceremonial sequences arose from a simple intention that is deeply meaningful to me: the wish that what I have studied, learned, and accumulated through experience may be used for the benefit of others.

In every life there are times of suffering, unease, fear, obstruction, or circumstances that cannot be fully explained. At such times, a person may need not only an answer, but also a refuge for the heart, encouragement, and a way to steady the mind in what is wholesome.

For this reason, I gathered and arranged these passages as a continuous ceremonial sequence: paying homage to the Triple Gem, taking refuge, asking forgiveness, recollecting the virtues of preceptors and teachers, inviting deities and guardians, dispelling what is harmful and sustaining what is beneficial, and finally proceeding to forgiveness, the dedication of merit, loving-kindness, and an invitation for all beings to rejoice in that goodness.

Viewed as a whole, the purpose of this ceremony is not fear. It is peace, safety, auspiciousness, and goodwill toward one another.

Even where the text speaks of dispelling or ending harm, its intention is not that such harm should return to injure anyone. On the contrary, it asks that harm cease, that enmity and resentment be calmed, that no being harm another, and that all beings find happiness and safety.

This is the central intention of this work:

May what is harmful end where it stands.
May what is beneficial remain and flourish.
May enmity be calmed.
May suffering be eased.
And may any goodness arising here not be confined to one person, but extend for the welfare and happiness of all living beings.

This text does not claim that words possess power over another person’s life, nor does it ask anyone to abandon reason, responsibility, or appropriate ways of solving problems. It is offered as another means of steadying the mind, recollecting goodness, restraining body, speech, and mind, and cultivating an intention that does not harm.

If this collection helps someone in distress find a place for the heart to rest, helps someone who is shaken regain mindfulness, helps people in conflict remember forgiveness, or inspires even one person to wish another well, then the intention behind this work has not been in vain.

Whatever goodness arises from studying, compiling, arranging, reciting, practising, or using this text with wholesome intention, I dedicate that goodness to peace, safety, and the welfare of all beings—without favouritism, rejection, or the wish that anyone should suffer in another’s place.

May there be peace.
May there be safety.
May what is beneficial flourish.
May every wholesome intention conduce to the welfare and happiness of all living beings.

Ajarn Pol Mahamaneejinda

Compiler and arranger

Objectives

This work was prepared to arrange the chants and ceremonial sequence systematically and continuously, making them convenient for study, recitation, and respectful use. Its principal objectives are:

  1. To recollect the Triple Gem and establish it as the refuge of the mind before beginning the ceremony.
  2. To express gratitude and respect toward preceptors, teachers, benefactors, and all who have offered guidance and support.
  3. To allow the practitioner to examine themselves and ask forgiveness for mistakes of body, speech, and mind, whether committed knowingly or unknowingly.
  4. To bring harmful influences, painful bonds, enmity, resentment, and causes of injury to an end, without wishing that harm return to any other person or living being.
  5. To sustain what is beneficial and wholesome, including what is grounded in the Buddha, the Dhamma, the Saṅgha, and the virtues of teachers.
  6. To ensure that the ceremony does not end merely with dispelling or restraint, but with forgiveness, the dedication of merit, loving-kindness, and goodwill toward all beings.
  7. To remind every user that the heart of the ceremony is not victory over or destruction of anyone, but the ending of harm and the growth of what is beneficial.
  8. To preserve and transmit the chants and ceremonial sequence in a systematic form that can be studied, checked, and continued without departing from the intention of the source text.

Above all, this work has one ultimate intention: may what has been prepared with wholesome intention help and support others; may harm subside, may what is beneficial grow, may those who suffer find peace, and may every goodness arising from this work conduce to the welfare and happiness of all living beings.

Source and Editorial Record

Title: Chant for Peace and Auspiciousness

Compiler and arranger: Ajarn Pol Mahamaneejinda

Nature of the work: A collection of traditional chants arranged into a continuous ceremonial sequence, with certain passages composed to explain the intention and connect the stages of practice. The work therefore does not claim that every passage is Buddhavacana or that all passages derive from a single canonical source.

Roman-script preparation: The Pāli was transliterated from the Pāli-in-Thai-script master and checked paragraph by paragraph against the Thai recitation edition for wording, pronunciation, and meaning.

Published master: The Thai edition on the Dhamma Gift Chant website.

Version policy: Any change to the wording, transliteration, pronunciation guide, or translation must be issued as a new dated version, while the previous version is retained for comparison.

Prepared as a Dhamma gift for study, recitation, peace, and goodwill toward all beings.
